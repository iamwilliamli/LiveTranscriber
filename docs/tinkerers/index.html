    • 02Front end

    Models never hear sound. They read log‑Mel frames.

    8 kHz4 kHz2 kHz1 kHz500 Hz
    Mel[80 × T]
    0 frames · 0.0 s · 10 ms hop
    1 s audio = 16,000 samples → 100 frames → ×8 conv subsampling → 12.5 encoder steps (80 ms each)
    1
    16 kHz mono PCM

    The mic delivers 48 kHz stereo. Every model wants 16,000 Float32 samples per second.

    2
    Short‑time Fourier transform

    25 ms windows, hopping every 10 ms, give 100 spectra per second.

    3
    Mel filterbank + log

    80 bins (Whisper) or 128 (Nemotron, Qwen3‑ASR, Whisper large‑v3). The bins are spaced like the cochlea: dense at low frequencies, sparse at high ones.

    4
    Encoder

    Conformer or Transformer blocks learn context: convolution captures local patterns, attention captures the whole utterance.

    Nobody cuts speech into phonemes by hand. Short overlapping windows turn the waveform into frames. The encoder learns what the sounds mean, and the decoder chooses the next text token.
  • Click “Use my mic” and say something. This is a real log‑Mel spectrogram computed in the browser.
  • That 80 × T picture is literally the input tensor. 100 columns per second; the encoder then merges every 8 into one step, so the model “thinks” at 12.5 Hz.
  • From here on, all the models differ only in how they decode.
    • 03Model families

    Similar encoders, four ways to decode

    Mel[F × T]→ conv subsample ×8→ Conformer / Transformer × N→ h₁ … hT′ what happens after ht is where the families split
    Streaming

    RNN‑T transducer

    “emit a token, or advance”
    Joint(ht, Pred(yu−1)) → y | ⟨blank⟩

    Blank means “nothing new in this frame”. Naturally streaming.

    Nemotron 3.5 · live
    Offline · fast

    TDT transducer

    “emit a token and skip Δt”
    → (yu, Δt ∈ {0…4})

    Predicts a duration too, so it skips frames and gets word timings for free.

    Parakeet V3 · 25 EU langs
    Offline · 30 s windows

    Encoder–decoder

    “cross‑attend, then autoregress”
    P(yi | Enc(X), y<i)

    Control tokens choose the language and task. Timestamps are tokens too.

    Whisper tiny → large‑v3
    Offline · LLM‑style

    Audio‑LLM

    “audio as tokens in a prompt”
    P(yi | [prompt; W·Enc(X)], y<i)

    A projector maps audio into the LLM's token space; then it's plain next‑token prediction.

    Qwen3‑ASRMOSS

    Apple SpeechAnalyzer

    A system black box: no app‑side tokens. You get partial → revision → final results with timing.

    Sortformer · Community‑1

    Diarization models output no text at all, only who × when. More on that in two slides.

  • The encoders look alike. The decoder contract is what changes.
  • Transducers are streaming‑native; TDT is the fast offline variant. Whisper is the classic encoder‑decoder. The newest ones are audio‑LLMs: audio becomes embeddings in an LLM prompt.
  • The app ships all four, and picks one by language and by live vs offline.
    • 04Live path · transducers

    At every frame: emit a token, or move on

    RNN‑T: blank advances time

    logits = Joint(ht, Pred(yu−1)) ∈ ℝ|V|+1

    ⟨blank⟩ = go to the next frame, add nothing. A BPE token (▁Meet, ing) is appended and fed back, and the same frame is tried again. Calls = T + U.

    TDT: predict the jump, too

    (yu, Δt) = argmax Ptok · argmax Pdur

    Each call also picks how many frames to skip: far fewer calls, and the jumps add up to word timestamps.

    Cache‑aware streaming Nemotron 3.5 · 0.6B

    Only the newest 320 ms is computed. Each layer caches attention K/V and conv state, so cost per step stays flat, even 2 hours into a meeting.

    320 ms → 5,120 samples → 32 Mel frames → 4 encoder steps → 24 cached layers
  • This is the decoding lattice: x is audio frames, y is tokens emitted so far.
  • RNN‑T: at each frame the joint network says either blank (go right) or a token (go up). 12 frames + 3 tokens = 15 decoder calls.
  • Toggle TDT: the model also predicts a duration and jumps. 5 calls instead of 15, and the jumps are the timestamps.
  • Bottom right: Nemotron only computes the new 320 ms; the rest comes from cache. That's why it runs live on a 6 GB iPhone.

    One token at a time, conditioned on all of the audio

    NEXT‑TOKEN DISTRIBUTION (illustrative)
  • Whisper: control tokens say language and task; timestamps are just tokens in the vocabulary.
  • Qwen3‑ASR: audio embeddings literally replace placeholder tokens in an LLM prompt. Look at the Chinese probabilities: 今 vs 金 vs 近 sound alike; the language model resolves them from context. That's why it handles dialects well.
  • MOSS goes further: one token stream carries text, time markers and speaker tags at once.
    • 06Who spoke when

    Speaker models output who and when, never words

    Sortformer · live

    streaming · 4 speakers

    Per‑frame sigmoid P(Sk | t) for four speaker slots. The slots are ordered by arrival time, which removes the permutation ambiguity. An arrival‑order speaker cache keeps S1 as S1 across chunks.

  • Diarization is a separate model family. It never transcribes.
  • Sortformer, live: four sigmoid outputs per frame. The trick is that speakers are sorted by who spoke first, so the training labels are unambiguous.
  • Community‑1, offline: windows → voiceprints → clustering, and then a join on time with Parakeet's word timestamps. That join is why TDT timing mattered.
    • 07After ASR

    The LLM reads the transcript, never the audio

    Once the text is final, a small local model writes the title, summary and tags. Apple Intelligence is used when the device has it. Everyone else (including China‑region iPhones) gets a GGUF model running through embedded llama.cpp.

    • Quantize everything: INT8 Core ML, 4‑bit MLX, Q5_0 and K‑quants. Memory, not FLOPs, is the real budget.
    • Models ship as Apple‑hosted on‑demand asset packs (Background Assets): pinned upstream revisions, SHA‑256 verified, downloaded only when you choose a model.
    • One shared domain layer for iPhone, Watch and a native Mac app that captures any meeting window via ScreenCaptureKit.
  • The hard part isn't the models; it's memory. Everything is quantized, and only one big model is resident at a time.
  • Four runtimes, because the best port of each model lives in a different one.
  • Models are distributed through Apple's own asset‑pack CDN, verified by checksum. No server of mine ever sees audio.
